# Big Bang Theory

The Big Bang theory is the prevailing cosmological model, holding that the universe expanded from an extremely hot, dense state around 13.8 billion years ago and has been cooling and growing ever since. It is the standard account of cosmic origins.

The idea grew from Georges Lemaitre's 1927 "primeval atom" proposal and Edwin Hubble's 1929 observation that distant galaxies recede faster the farther away they lie, implying an expanding cosmos rooted in [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]]. Decisive support came in 1965 when Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson detected the cosmic microwave background, the relic radiation predicted by the model. Confirming these predictions through observation made the theory a landmark of the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]], later refined by the deep-field images of the [[space/hubble-space-telescope|Hubble Space Telescope]].

## Open Questions

Today the [[space/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] is probing the universe's earliest galaxies to test how the first structures formed. Major puzzles remain, including the nature of dark matter and dark energy and the physics of the first instant, where the theory meets the frontiers of [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]].
