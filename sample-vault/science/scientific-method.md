# Scientific Method

The scientific method is the disciplined cycle of observation, hypothesis, experiment, and revision that transformed natural philosophy into modern science. This note gathers the landmark discoveries in this vault that the method made possible.

Rooted in the empiricism of Francis Bacon and sharpened during the seventeenth-century Scientific Revolution, the method insists that claims about nature be testable, measurable, and open to refutation. Its power shows in the discoveries below, each of which overturned an older picture of the world by surviving exactly that kind of scrutiny.

## Foundations of classical physics

- [[science/newtonian-mechanics]] — Newton's laws of motion and universal gravitation, the first grand unification of heaven and earth.
- [[science/calculus]] — the mathematics of continuous change, developed by Newton and Leibniz to make mechanics precise.
- [[science/electromagnetism]] — Faraday's fields and Maxwell's equations, joining electricity, magnetism, and light.
- [[science/thermodynamics]] — the laws of energy and entropy, distilled from the practical study of steam engines.

## Matter and life

- [[science/periodic-table]] — Mendeleev's 1869 arrangement of the elements, bold enough to predict undiscovered ones.
- [[science/radioactivity]] — Marie Curie's and Rutherford's window into the unstable atom.
- [[science/theory-of-evolution]] — Darwin's account of descent with modification through natural selection.
- [[science/germ-theory]] — Pasteur's and Koch's demonstration that microbes, not miasmas, cause disease.
- [[science/dna-double-helix]] — the 1953 structure that explained heredity at the molecular level.

## The modern revolutions

- [[science/special-relativity]] — Einstein's 1905 rethinking of space, time, and simultaneity.
- [[science/general-relativity]] — gravity recast as the curvature of spacetime, confirmed by the 1919 eclipse expedition.
- [[science/quantum-mechanics]] — the probabilistic physics of atoms, from Planck's quanta to Heisenberg's uncertainty.
- [[science/big-bang-theory]] — the expanding universe, traced from Hubble's redshifts to the cosmic microwave background.
