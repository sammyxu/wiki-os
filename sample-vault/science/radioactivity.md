# Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of particles or energy from unstable atomic nuclei as they decay toward more stable configurations. Its discovery opened a window into the atom itself and reshaped physics, chemistry, and medicine.

Henri Becquerel noticed in 1896 that uranium salts fogged photographic plates, and [[people/marie-curie|Marie Curie]] — who coined the term radioactivity — went on to isolate polonium and radium, winning Nobel Prizes in both physics and chemistry. Systematic measurement and controlled experiment made the phenomenon a model of the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]]. The new elements found their place in the [[science/periodic-table|periodic table]], while the underlying decay processes were eventually explained by [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]].

Radioactivity also confirmed the deep link between mass and energy expressed by [[science/special-relativity|special relativity]], since nuclear decay converts a tiny amount of mass into released energy.

## Types of Decay

- Alpha: emission of a helium nucleus
- Beta: conversion of a neutron to a proton with an emitted electron
- Gamma: release of high-energy electromagnetic radiation
