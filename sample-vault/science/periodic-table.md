# Periodic Table

The periodic table organizes the chemical elements by atomic number into rows and columns that reveal recurring patterns in their properties. It is one of the most compact and predictive summaries in all of science.

Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the known elements in 1869 so that similar behaviors lined up in vertical groups, and he boldly left gaps for elements not yet discovered, correctly predicting their properties. This convergence of observation and prediction embodies the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]]. The table's structure was later explained by [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], which showed that electron shells determine chemical behavior.

The discovery of [[science/radioactivity|radioactivity]] extended the table further, as [[people/marie-curie|Marie Curie]] isolated new elements such as polonium and radium and helped clarify how heavy, unstable nuclei fit within the periodic order.

## Periodicity

- Rows (periods) fill successive electron shells
- Columns (groups) share valence electrons and reactivity
- Trends in atomic size, ionization energy, and electronegativity repeat predictably
