# DNA Double Helix

The double helix is the twisted-ladder structure of DNA, worked out in 1953, which revealed how living things store and copy hereditary information. Its discovery founded the modern science of molecular biology.

James Watson and Francis Crick built their model at Cambridge, but it rested on the X-ray diffraction work of [[people/rosalind-franklin|Rosalind Franklin]], whose famous "Photograph 51" captured the helical geometry with striking clarity. The structure immediately suggested a copying mechanism: the two strands, held together by complementary base pairs, can unzip so each serves as a template for a new partner. This finally gave a physical basis for the inheritance that [[science/theory-of-evolution|Darwin's theory of evolution]] required, and demonstrated the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]] at its most powerful — a structure whose form directly implied its function.

## Legacy

- The genetic code linking DNA triplets to amino acids, cracked through the 1960s
- Recombinant DNA and biotechnology, from the 1970s onward
- Genome sequencing, culminating in the Human Genome Project
- CRISPR gene editing, which builds directly on this molecular understanding
