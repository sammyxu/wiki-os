# General Relativity

General relativity is [[people/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]]'s 1915 theory of gravitation, which describes gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime by mass and energy. It remains the best available description of gravity a century after its publication.

Einstein spent a decade extending his 1905 [[science/special-relativity|special relativity]] to accelerated frames, guided by the equivalence principle — the insight that falling freely feels the same as floating in empty space. The resulting field equations superseded [[science/newtonian-mechanics|Newtonian mechanics]] as a theory of gravity, correctly explaining the anomalous precession of Mercury's orbit. The theory became a textbook case of the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]] when Arthur Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition measured starlight bending around the Sun by exactly the predicted amount.

## Predictions and Consequences

- Black holes, regions where curvature traps even light, first described by Karl Schwarzschild in 1916
- Gravitational waves, detected directly by LIGO in 2015
- An expanding universe, the foundation of the [[science/big-bang-theory|Big Bang theory]]
- Gravitational time dilation, which GPS satellites must correct for daily

Reconciling the theory with [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] remains one of the deepest open problems in physics.
