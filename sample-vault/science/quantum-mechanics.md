# Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is the theory describing nature at atomic and subatomic scales, where energy comes in discrete packets and outcomes are inherently probabilistic. It is among the most precisely tested theories in the history of science.

The quantum era opened in 1900 when Max Planck introduced energy quanta to explain blackbody radiation, and [[people/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]] applied the idea to light itself in 1905. Between 1925 and 1927, Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrodinger, Max Born, and Paul Dirac built the full mathematical framework, with Heisenberg's uncertainty principle setting hard limits on what can be simultaneously known. Later, [[people/richard-feynman|Richard Feynman]] reformulated the theory through path integrals and co-developed quantum electrodynamics, whose predictions match experiment to better than one part in a billion — a triumph of the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]].

## Impact Beyond Physics

Quantum theory explains chemical bonding, the [[science/periodic-table|periodic table]], and the behavior of semiconductors that made the [[computing/transistor|transistor]] possible. Its unresolved tension with [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]] continues to drive the search for a theory of quantum gravity.
