# Theory of Evolution

The theory of evolution explains how living species change across generations through the differential survival and reproduction of inherited variations. It reframed biology around a single unifying idea: all organisms share common ancestry.

Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species in 1859, arguing that natural selection acting on heritable variation drives adaptation over deep time. [[people/charles-darwin|Darwin]] developed the idea alongside Alfred Russel Wallace, and its explanatory power grew once genetics revealed how traits are inherited. The theory rests firmly on the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]], drawing on fossils, comparative anatomy, and later molecular evidence from the [[science/dna-double-helix|structure of DNA]].

Evolution is a textbook case of [[systems/emergence|emergence]]: complex, well-adapted forms arise from simple local rules of variation and selection without any guiding plan. Population-level order appears through [[systems/self-organization|self-organization]] rather than deliberate design.

## Natural Selection

Individuals vary; some variations improve survival or reproduction; those traits become more common over generations. Mutation supplies novelty, while selection, genetic drift, and gene flow shape which variants persist through time.
