# Electromagnetism

Electromagnetism is the unified theory of electricity, magnetism, and light, assembled over the nineteenth century from tabletop experiments and completed as a set of four elegant field equations.

Hans Christian Ørsted noticed in 1820 that a current deflects a compass needle, and Michael Faraday showed the converse in 1831: a changing magnetic field induces a current, the principle behind every generator. Faraday, a bookbinder's apprentice with no formal mathematics, imagined invisible lines of force filling space; James Clerk Maxwell translated that intuition into equations in the 1860s and found they predicted waves traveling at the speed of light. Heinrich Hertz confirmed those waves in 1887, a striking vindication of the [[science/scientific-method]] as prediction preceding observation.

## Legacy

Maxwell's equations seeded two revolutions. Their fixed speed of light led [[people/albert-einstein|Albert Einstein]] to [[science/special-relativity]], while puzzles about radiation from hot objects opened the door to [[science/quantum-mechanics]]. Radio, radar, and electric power all trace back to Faraday's coil.
