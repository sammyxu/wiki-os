# Newtonian Mechanics

Newtonian mechanics is the framework of three laws of motion and universal gravitation that governed physics for more than two centuries after its publication in 1687.

In the *Principia Mathematica*, [[people/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]] showed that the fall of an apple and the orbit of the Moon obey the same inverse-square law of gravity, unifying celestial and terrestrial motion for the first time. To state the laws precisely he needed the new mathematics of [[science/calculus]], and his insistence on deriving predictions from principles and checking them against observation made the *Principia* a model of the [[science/scientific-method]]. Edmond Halley used the theory to predict the 1758 return of his comet, a triumph Newton did not live to see.

## The three laws

- A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a force.
- Force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma).
- Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
