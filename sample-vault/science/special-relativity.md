# Special Relativity

Special relativity is the theory of how space and time behave for observers moving at constant velocity, resting on the principle that the speed of light is the same for everyone. It revealed that measurements of time and distance depend on the observer's motion.

Albert Einstein introduced the theory in 1905, discarding the notion of absolute time and reconciling mechanics with [[science/electromagnetism|electromagnetism]]. [[people/albert-einstein|Einstein]] showed that moving clocks run slow and moving lengths contract, and that mass and energy are equivalent through E = mc². The reasoning and experiment behind it followed the tradition of the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]], and it superseded [[science/newtonian-mechanics|Newtonian mechanics]] at speeds approaching that of light without discarding it in everyday life.

A decade later Einstein extended these ideas to gravity and acceleration in [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]], reshaping our picture of the cosmos.

## Key Consequences

- Time dilation and length contraction
- The relativity of simultaneity
- Mass–energy equivalence, E = mc²
