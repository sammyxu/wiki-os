# Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics is the science of energy, heat, and entropy, born from the practical question of how much work a steam engine can extract from burning coal.

Sadi Carnot's 1824 analysis of ideal engines set an absolute limit on efficiency, and by mid-century James Joule, Rudolf Clausius, and William Thomson had forged the results into two great laws: energy is conserved, and entropy never decreases in an isolated system. Ludwig Boltzmann then explained entropy statistically, as a count of microscopic arrangements, a bold hypothesis about unseen atoms that the [[science/scientific-method]] eventually vindicated. Puzzles about thermal radiation later pushed Max Planck toward [[science/quantum-mechanics]].

## Entropy beyond physics

Boltzmann's formula found an unexpected echo in 1948, when [[people/claude-shannon|Claude Shannon]] used the same mathematics to measure information, founding [[systems/information-theory]]. The parallel runs deep: erasing a bit of information has a real thermodynamic cost, and living systems maintain their order by exporting entropy, a theme picked up in studies of [[systems/emergence]].
