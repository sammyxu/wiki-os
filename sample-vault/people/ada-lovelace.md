# Ada Lovelace

Ada Lovelace (1815-1852) was an English mathematician who wrote the first published computer algorithm, describing how [[computing/analytical-engine|the Analytical Engine]] could compute Bernoulli numbers. She is widely regarded as the first computer programmer.

## Life

The only legitimate child of the poet Lord Byron, Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, was raised by her mother on a strict diet of mathematics and tutored by Mary Somerville and Augustus De Morgan. In 1833 she met [[people/charles-babbage]], beginning a long collaboration. Her 1843 translation of Luigi Menabrea's paper on the engine, expanded with her famous Notes A through G, contained the step-by-step algorithm that secured her place in the [[computing/history-of-computing|history of computing]]. She died of cancer at just thirty-six.

## Legacy

Lovelace saw further than her contemporaries: she argued the engine could manipulate symbols and even compose music, not merely crunch numbers, calling her approach "poetical science." Her claim that a machine "has no pretensions to originate anything" was later debated by [[people/alan-turing]] as "Lady Lovelace's objection" in his work on [[computing/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]]. The Ada programming language, named in 1980, honors her.
