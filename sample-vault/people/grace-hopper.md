# Grace Hopper

Grace Hopper (1906-1992) was an American computer scientist and United States Navy rear admiral who pioneered the compiler and championed machine-independent programming languages. Her work made programming accessible far beyond mathematicians.

## Life

Hopper earned a PhD in mathematics from Yale in 1934 and taught at Vassar before joining the Navy WAVES in 1943, where she programmed the Harvard Mark I under Howard Aiken. In 1949 she joined the Eckert-Mauchly Computer Corporation, founded by the builders of [[computing/eniac|ENIAC]], and worked on UNIVAC I, one of the first commercial machines built on the [[computing/stored-program-concept|stored-program concept]] articulated by [[people/john-von-neumann]]. There she wrote the A-0 system in 1952, the first compiler, and later FLOW-MATIC, whose English-like syntax directly shaped COBOL in 1959.

## Legacy

Hopper popularized the term "debugging" after her team taped a moth found in the Mark II into its logbook in 1947. Retiring in 1986 as the Navy's oldest active-duty officer, she spent her final years lecturing with a nanosecond-length piece of wire in hand. She remains a touchstone in the [[computing/history-of-computing|history of computing]], an inspiration to later software pioneers such as [[people/margaret-hamilton]].
