# Norbert Wiener

Norbert Wiener (1894-1964) was an American mathematician and philosopher who founded [[systems/cybernetics|cybernetics]], the study of control and communication in animals and machines.

A child prodigy who earned his Harvard PhD at eighteen, Wiener spent his career at MIT working on stochastic processes, Brownian motion, and harmonic analysis. His wartime work on anti-aircraft fire control convinced him that purposeful behavior in machines and organisms alike depends on [[systems/feedback-loops|feedback loops]], an insight he generalized in his 1948 book "Cybernetics."

## Life

Born in Columbia, Missouri, Wiener was educated intensively by his father, a Harvard philologist, before studying zoology and philosophy at Harvard and mathematics under Bertrand Russell and G. H. Hardy in Cambridge. At MIT from 1919, he collaborated widely across engineering and physiology, and his statistical theory of communication developed in parallel with the [[systems/information-theory|information theory]] of [[people/claude-shannon|Claude Shannon]].

## Legacy

Cybernetics shaped control engineering, neuroscience, and early ideas about [[systems/self-organization|self-organization]] in complex systems. Wiener was also an early moral voice on automation: "The Human Use of Human Beings" (1950) anticipated debates now central to the [[philosophy/ethics-of-technology|ethics of technology]].
