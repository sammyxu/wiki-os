# Alan Turing

Alan Turing (1912-1954) was a British mathematician and logician, widely considered the founder of theoretical computer science. His 1936 paper introduced the [[computing/turing-machine|Turing machine]], an abstract model that defines what it means for a problem to be computable.

## Life

Educated at King's College, Cambridge, and Princeton, Turing answered Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem by proving that no general algorithm can decide the truth of every mathematical statement. During the Second World War he led Hut 8 at Bletchley Park, where his bombe machines drove the [[computing/enigma-codebreaking|breaking of the Enigma cipher]], shortening the war by an estimated two years. Afterward he designed the ACE computer and helped shape the [[computing/stored-program-concept|stored-program concept]] alongside contemporaries such as [[people/john-von-neumann]]. Prosecuted in 1952 for homosexuality, he died two years later; the British government formally pardoned him in 2013.

## Legacy

Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" proposed the imitation game, now called the Turing test, framing the field of [[computing/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] decades before it had a name. His ideas on computation influenced thinkers from [[people/claude-shannon]] onward, and the Turing Award remains computing's highest honor.
