# Carl Sagan

Carl Sagan (1934-1996) was an American astronomer, planetary scientist, and science communicator who brought the wonder of the cosmos to millions and curated the Golden Record carried by the [[space/voyager-program]].

A professor at Cornell University, Sagan contributed to NASA's Mariner, Viking, and Voyager missions, and his early work on the greenhouse effect explained the searing surface temperature of Venus. His 1980 television series *Cosmos* became one of the most-watched science programs in history, and books like *The Demon-Haunted World* championed skepticism and the [[science/scientific-method]] as a candle in the dark.

## Life

Born in Brooklyn, New York, Sagan earned his doctorate at the University of Chicago and spent most of his career at Cornell. He co-founded the Planetary Society in 1980 to advance [[space/space-exploration]] and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence.

## Legacy

Sagan's "Pale Blue Dot" reflection, inspired by a 1990 Voyager 1 photograph of Earth, remains a touchstone of cosmic perspective. His advocacy helped sustain public support for robotic missions such as the [[space/mars-rovers]], and the Voyager Golden Record he curated still carries Earth's greeting to the stars.
