# John von Neumann

John von Neumann (1903-1957) was a Hungarian-American polymath whose work spans computer architecture, game theory, quantum mechanics, and nuclear physics. The design of nearly every modern computer traces back to his 1945 report on the EDVAC.

## Life

Born in Budapest, von Neumann published his axiomatization of set theory as a young man and, in 1932, the mathematical foundations of [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]]. He joined the Institute for Advanced Study at Princeton in 1933, where he later crossed paths with [[people/alan-turing]] during Turing's doctoral studies. His 1944 book with Oskar Morgenstern, "Theory of Games and Economic Behavior," founded [[systems/game-theory|game theory]]. Consulting on [[computing/eniac|ENIAC]], he wrote the "First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC" in 1945, the canonical statement of the [[computing/stored-program-concept|stored-program concept]] now called the von Neumann architecture.

## Legacy

Beyond computing, von Neumann contributed to the Manhattan Project, invented cellular automata, and described self-replicating machines decades before molecular biology confirmed the idea, prefiguring the study of [[systems/self-organization|self-organization]]. Colleagues considered him the fastest mind of his generation; he died of cancer at fifty-three.
