# Tim Berners-Lee

Tim Berners-Lee (born 1955) is a British computer scientist who invented the [[computing/world-wide-web|World Wide Web]] while working at CERN, combining [[computing/hypertext|hypertext]] with the Internet to create a global information space.

In March 1989 he circulated a proposal for a linked document system to manage information at CERN; by the end of 1990 he had written the first browser, the first server, and the core specifications for HTML, HTTP, and URLs. Crucially, in 1993 CERN released the technology royalty-free, a decision that echoes the spirit of the [[computing/open-source-movement|open source movement]] and let the Web spread without restriction.

## Life

Born in London to parents who both worked on the Ferranti Mark 1, Berners-Lee studied physics at Oxford. His Web built on decades of prior work, from the [[computing/arpanet|ARPANET]] to the hypertext demonstrations of [[people/douglas-engelbart|Douglas Engelbart]]. In 1994 he founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT to steward open standards.

## Legacy

The Web became the defining example of [[systems/network-effects|network effects]]: each new page and user made the whole more valuable. Knighted in 2004 and awarded the 2016 Turing Award, Berners-Lee continues to advocate for an open, decentralized Web.
