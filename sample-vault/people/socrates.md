# Socrates

Socrates (c. 470-399 BC) was a classical Greek philosopher from Athens, widely regarded as a founder of Western philosophy and the originator of a style of questioning that still bears his name.

Socrates wrote nothing himself; we know him chiefly through the dialogues of his student Plato and the recollections of Xenophon. In the marketplace of Athens he practiced the [[philosophy/socratic-method]], relentlessly cross-examining claims about justice, virtue, and knowledge until hidden contradictions surfaced. His insistence that he knew only that he knew nothing made the limits of human knowledge a central problem of [[philosophy/epistemology]].

## Life

Born in Athens around 470 BC, Socrates served as a hoplite in the Peloponnesian War before devoting himself to philosophy. In 399 BC he was tried for impiety and corrupting the youth, convicted, and executed by drinking hemlock, refusing offers to escape.

## Legacy

Through Plato and Aristotle, Socrates shaped nearly every later movement among the [[philosophy/schools-of-thought]], from [[philosophy/stoicism]] to modern ethics. His declaration that "the unexamined life is not worth living" remains a defining statement of the philosophical vocation, later echoed by admirers such as [[people/marcus-aurelius]].
