# Charles Babbage

Charles Babbage (1791-1871) was an English polymath who designed the Difference Engine and the [[computing/analytical-engine|Analytical Engine]], the first design for a general-purpose programmable computer. He is often called the "father of the computer."

## Life

Born in London, Babbage co-founded the Analytical Society at Cambridge to champion Leibniz's notation for [[science/calculus|calculus]], and later held the Lucasian Professorship of Mathematics from 1828 to 1839. Frustrated by error-ridden mathematical tables, he proposed the Difference Engine in 1822 to mechanize their calculation. By 1837 he had conceived the far more ambitious Analytical Engine, with a "mill" for arithmetic, a "store" for memory, and punched-card input borrowed from the Jacquard loom. His collaborator [[people/ada-lovelace]] published the most insightful account of its powers.

## Legacy

Though neither engine was completed in his lifetime, Babbage's separation of processing from memory foreshadowed the [[computing/stored-program-concept|stored-program concept]] at the heart of modern machines, earning him a founding place in the [[computing/history-of-computing|history of computing]]. In 1991 the Science Museum in London built his Difference Engine No. 2 from the original plans, and it worked flawlessly.
