# Margaret Hamilton

Margaret Hamilton (born 1936) is an American software engineer who led the team that wrote the onboard flight software for NASA's Apollo spacecraft at the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory.

Hamilton's team designed the Apollo Guidance Computer software with rigorous error detection and priority scheduling. During the [[space/apollo-11]] descent in July 1969, the computer threw 1202 and 1201 alarms as it was overloaded with data; because her software could shed low-priority tasks and keep the critical landing programs running, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin touched down safely. She is also credited with popularizing the term "software engineering" to give the young discipline the standing it deserved in the [[computing/history-of-computing]].

## Life

Born Margaret Heafield in Paoli, Indiana, she studied mathematics at Earlham College before joining MIT, where she worked on weather prediction and the SAGE air defense system prior to the [[space/apollo-program]]. She later founded two companies focused on ultra-reliable software design.

## Legacy

Hamilton received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. Her insistence that flight software be tested against human error shaped modern safety-critical engineering, and she stands alongside pioneers like [[people/grace-hopper]] and [[people/katherine-johnson]] in computing and spaceflight history.
