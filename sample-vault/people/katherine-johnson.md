# Katherine Johnson

Katherine Johnson (1918-2020) was an American mathematician whose orbital trajectory calculations at NASA helped launch the first Americans into space and land astronauts on the Moon.

Johnson joined the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA) in 1953, working in the segregated West Area Computing unit at Langley. During the [[space/space-race]] she computed the trajectory for Alan Shepard's 1961 Freedom 7 flight, and in 1962 John Glenn famously asked her to verify the electronic computer's numbers by hand before his Friendship 7 orbital mission. Her rendezvous and return calculations later supported the [[space/apollo-program]], including the flight of [[space/apollo-11]].

## Life

Born in White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia, Johnson showed early brilliance in mathematics and graduated from West Virginia State College at eighteen. She worked at Langley from 1953 until her retirement in 1986, co-authoring dozens of technical reports on spaceflight trajectories.

## Legacy

Johnson received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015, and the 2016 book and film *Hidden Figures* brought her story to a global audience. NASA named its Computational Research Facility at Langley in her honor, and her career remains a landmark in the history of [[space/space-exploration]].
