# Claude Shannon

Claude Shannon (1916-2001) was an American mathematician and electrical engineer whose 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" founded [[systems/information-theory|information theory]] and gave the world the bit as a unit of measure.

Shannon's 1937 MIT master's thesis showed that Boolean algebra could describe relay switching circuits, laying a logical foundation for digital design. During World War II he worked on cryptography at Bell Labs, where he crossed paths with [[people/alan-turing|Alan Turing]], who was visiting from Britain; the two discussed the possibility of machines that could think.

## Life

Born in Petoskey, Michigan, Shannon studied at the University of Michigan and MIT before joining Bell Telephone Laboratories in 1941. His communication theory defined channel capacity and showed that error-free transmission is possible below that limit. A playful tinkerer, he built a maze-solving mechanical mouse named Theseus and juggling machines, and he helped seed early work in [[computing/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].

## Legacy

Every compressed file, error-correcting code, and noisy-channel link in modern networks rests on Shannon's framework. His ideas flowed into the [[systems/cybernetics|cybernetics]] circle around [[people/norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]], and entropy remains the common currency of communication, computation, and physics.
