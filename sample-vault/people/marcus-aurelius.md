# Marcus Aurelius

Marcus Aurelius (121-180 AD) was Roman emperor from 161 to 180 and a Stoic philosopher whose private notebook, the *Meditations*, became one of the most influential works of practical philosophy ever written.

The last of the so-called Five Good Emperors, Marcus ruled through plague and near-constant war on the Danube frontier. In camp at night he wrote reflections to himself in Greek, applying the teachings of [[philosophy/stoicism]] — especially those of the former slave Epictetus — to anger, grief, duty, and death. Never intended for publication, the *Meditations* reads like a personal commonplace book, a striking ancient ancestor of [[writing/commonplace-books]].

## Life

Born in Rome in 121 AD, Marcus was adopted into the imperial succession by Antoninus Pius at Hadrian's direction. He became emperor in 161, initially co-ruling with Lucius Verus, and spent much of his reign campaigning against Germanic tribes until his death at Vindobona in 180.

## Legacy

The *Meditations* kept Stoic ethics alive among the great [[philosophy/schools-of-thought]] and fueled the philosophy's modern revival. Marcus embodied the ideal of the philosopher-ruler tracing back through Plato to [[people/socrates]], proving that reflective self-examination could coexist with the burdens of power.
