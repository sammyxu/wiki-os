# Charles Darwin

Charles Darwin (1809-1882) was an English naturalist whose book *On the Origin of Species* (1859) transformed biology by explaining how species change over time through natural selection. His work remains the foundation of the modern [[science/theory-of-evolution|theory of evolution]].

Between 1831 and 1836 Darwin sailed aboard HMS *Beagle*, collecting specimens and observations across South America and the Galápagos Islands. The variation he documented among finches, mockingbirds, and tortoises seeded a decades-long inquiry, conducted with the patient observation and testing characteristic of the [[science/scientific-method]] and of [[philosophy/empiricism|empiricist]] thinking.

## Life

Born in Shrewsbury in 1809, Darwin abandoned medicine at Edinburgh and theology at Cambridge for natural history. After the *Beagle* voyage he settled at Down House in Kent, where he spent twenty years amassing evidence—on barnacles, pigeons, and orchids—before a letter from Alfred Russel Wallace prompted joint publication in 1858 and the *Origin* the following year.

## Legacy

Darwin's central insight, descent with modification driven by natural selection, was later united with genetics in the modern synthesis, and the discovery of the [[science/dna-double-helix|DNA double helix]] revealed the molecular basis of the heredity his theory required. He was buried in Westminster Abbey in 1882.
