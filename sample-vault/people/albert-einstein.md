# Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein (1879-1955) was a German-born theoretical physicist who reshaped our understanding of space, time, and gravity with the theories of [[science/special-relativity|special relativity]] and [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]].

In his "miracle year" of 1905, while working as a patent clerk in Bern, Einstein published papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, and special relativity, resolving tensions between [[science/newtonian-mechanics|Newtonian mechanics]] and Maxwell's [[science/electromagnetism]]. A decade later, general relativity recast gravity as the curvature of spacetime, superseding the framework of [[people/isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]] while preserving it as a limiting case.

## Life

Born in Ulm in 1879, Einstein studied at the ETH in Zurich and held professorships in Prague and Berlin. He received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for the photoelectric effect, a founding result of [[science/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], a theory whose probabilistic interpretation he nonetheless famously resisted. Fleeing Nazi Germany in 1933, he settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, where he worked until his death in 1955.

## Legacy

Confirmed by the 1919 eclipse expedition and, a century later, by gravitational-wave detections, Einstein's relativity underpins modern cosmology and even everyday GPS. His name has become a byword for scientific genius.
