# Douglas Engelbart

Douglas Engelbart (1925-2013) was an American engineer and inventor who created the computer mouse and, in the 1968 "Mother of All Demos," previewed nearly every element of modern interactive computing.

At his Augmentation Research Center at Stanford Research Institute, Engelbart built NLS (the oN-Line System), a working environment with [[computing/hypertext|hypertext]] linking, on-screen editing, windows, and real-time collaboration. His guiding mission, set out in the 1962 report "Augmenting Human Intellect," was to use computers to boost collective [[writing/knowledge-work|knowledge work]] rather than merely automate calculation.

## Life

Born in Portland, Oregon, Engelbart served as a Navy radar technician in World War II and earned a PhD in electrical engineering at Berkeley in 1955. His SRI lab became the second node on the [[computing/arpanet|ARPANET]] in 1969, and his December 9, 1968 demonstration in San Francisco stunned an audience of a thousand computer professionals.

## Legacy

Alumni of Engelbart's lab carried his ideas to Xerox PARC, where they shaped the graphical [[computing/personal-computer|personal computer]]; his hypertext vision later resurfaced in the [[computing/world-wide-web|World Wide Web]] of [[people/tim-berners-lee|Tim Berners-Lee]]. He received the Turing Award in 1997.
