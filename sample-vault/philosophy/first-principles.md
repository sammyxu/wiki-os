# First Principles

First-principles thinking is the practice of breaking a problem down to its most basic, self-evident truths and reasoning upward from there, rather than arguing by analogy or inherited convention. The approach runs from ancient philosophy straight into modern engineering.

Aristotle defined a first principle in the *Posterior Analytics* as "the first basis from which a thing is known," and Euclid's *Elements* (c. 300 BC) showed the method's power by deriving all of geometry from a handful of axioms. René Descartes rebuilt his entire worldview from the single certainty "I think, therefore I am," a landmark of [[philosophy/rationalism]] among the classical [[philosophy/schools-of-thought]]. The [[philosophy/socratic-method]] is its dialectical cousin: systematic questioning that strips away assumptions until only defensible foundations remain.

## From Axioms to Rockets

Isaac Newton's [[science/newtonian-mechanics|laws of motion]] demonstrated the method in science, deducing planetary orbits from three compact principles. In engineering, reasoning from the raw physics and material costs of spaceflight — rather than from historical launch prices — motivated the development of [[space/reusable-rockets|reusable rockets]], a frequently cited modern example of first-principles analysis.
