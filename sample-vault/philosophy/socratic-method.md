# Socratic Method

The Socratic method is a form of cooperative inquiry in which a questioner exposes contradictions in a partner's beliefs through a disciplined sequence of questions rather than lectures or assertions.

The method takes its name from [[people/socrates|Socrates]], the Athenian philosopher of the fifth century BCE who claimed to know nothing and taught nothing directly, yet reshaped Western thought through conversation. We know his practice mainly through Plato's dialogues — the *Euthyphro*, *Meno*, and *Republic* — where the technique of refutation, or *elenchus*, dismantles confident definitions of piety, virtue, and justice. As a way of testing what we really know, it remains a working tool of [[philosophy/epistemology|epistemology]], and it anchors the classical tradition surveyed in [[philosophy/schools-of-thought|Schools of Thought]].

## How the Method Works

- Ask for a definition of a contested concept, such as courage or justice.
- Draw out the consequences of the answer through further questions.
- Surface a counterexample or contradiction, prompting a revised definition.
- Repeat, moving the discussion closer to [[philosophy/first-principles|first principles]].
