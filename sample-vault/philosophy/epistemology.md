# Epistemology

Epistemology is the branch of philosophy that studies knowledge itself: what it is, where it comes from, and how beliefs can be justified. Its guiding questions reach back to Plato's *Theaetetus*, which examined the classic definition of knowledge as justified true belief.

The field's founding spirit is [[people/socrates]], whose relentless questioning exposed how little his interlocutors could actually justify. In the early modern period the debate split the [[philosophy/schools-of-thought]] into two camps: [[philosophy/rationalism]], with René Descartes seeking certainty in pure reason, and [[philosophy/empiricism]], with John Locke and David Hume tracing all knowledge to sense experience. Immanuel Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781) attempted a synthesis of the two.

## Modern Problems

In 1963 Edmund Gettier published a three-page paper showing that justified true belief can fall short of knowledge, reigniting the field. Contemporary epistemologists study skepticism, testimony, and the reliability of our belief-forming processes — questions with direct bearing on how the [[science/scientific-method|scientific method]] earns its authority as a way of knowing.
