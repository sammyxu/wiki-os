# Stoicism

Stoicism is a Hellenistic school of philosophy that teaches equanimity through a disciplined distinction between what is within our control and what is not.

Founded by Zeno of Citium in Athens around 300 BCE and systematized by Chrysippus, the school held that virtue is the only true good and that destructive emotions arise from errors in judgment. Its best-known voices are the Roman Stoics: Seneca, the former slave Epictetus, and the emperor [[people/marcus-aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]], whose private notebook, the *Meditations*, is still widely read. The Stoics traced their moral seriousness back to [[people/socrates|Socrates]], and the school stands alongside the other traditions gathered in [[philosophy/schools-of-thought|Schools of Thought]].

## Core Practices

- The dichotomy of control: act on what depends on you, accept what does not.
- Negative visualization (*premeditatio malorum*): rehearse setbacks in advance to blunt their sting.
- Evening journaling and self-examination, in the spirit of the *Meditations*.
- Viewing events "from above" to shrink personal grievances to cosmic scale.
