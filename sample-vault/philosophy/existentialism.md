# Existentialism

Existentialism is the philosophical movement holding that individuals must create meaning through their own free choices, because no external essence or cosmic order supplies it for them. Its central themes are freedom, authenticity, anxiety, and responsibility.

Søren Kierkegaard, writing in the 1840s, is usually named its forerunner, with Friedrich Nietzsche a second major precursor. The movement crystallized in mid-twentieth-century France: Jean-Paul Sartre's 1945 lecture *Existentialism Is a Humanism* summarized the creed as "existence precedes essence," while Simone de Beauvoir applied it to ethics and gender and Albert Camus explored the absurd. Within the broader map of [[philosophy/schools-of-thought]], it defined a sharp alternative to system-building rationalism and to calculating moralities like [[philosophy/utilitarianism]].

## Key Themes

- Radical freedom: humans are "condemned to be free," in Sartre's phrase.
- Authenticity versus bad faith, the denial of one's own agency.
- Anxiety (Kierkegaard's *Angest*) as the felt weight of choice.
- Consciousness and subjectivity, themes it shares with the [[philosophy/philosophy-of-mind|philosophy of mind]].

Its stress on how to live echoes ancient practical philosophies such as [[philosophy/stoicism]], though it rejects their faith in a rational cosmic order.
