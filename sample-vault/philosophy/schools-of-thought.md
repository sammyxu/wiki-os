# Schools of Thought

Philosophy is less a single subject than a family of rival traditions, each offering its own method for asking what is true, what is good, and how we should live. This note maps the major schools and thinking tools collected in this vault.

The entries below fall roughly into three groups: classical methods of inquiry, theories of knowledge, and ethical frameworks whose questions resurface in modern debates about minds and machines.

## Classical Methods and Practices

- [[philosophy/socratic-method|Socratic Method]] — question-driven dialogue as practiced by [[people/socrates|Socrates]] in fifth-century BCE Athens.
- [[philosophy/stoicism|Stoicism]] — the Hellenistic school of Zeno, Epictetus, and [[people/marcus-aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]], centered on virtue and what lies within our control.
- [[philosophy/first-principles|First Principles]] — Aristotle's habit of reasoning up from foundational truths rather than by analogy.
- [[philosophy/occams-razor|Occam's Razor]] — William of Ockham's rule that explanations should not multiply assumptions beyond necessity.

## Knowledge and Reason

- [[philosophy/epistemology|Epistemology]] — the general theory of knowledge: what it is, and how belief becomes justified.
- [[philosophy/empiricism|Empiricism]] — Locke and Hume's claim that knowledge begins in sense experience.
- [[philosophy/rationalism|Rationalism]] — Descartes' rival claim that reason alone can grasp necessary truths.

## Ethics, Minds, and Machines

- [[philosophy/utilitarianism|Utilitarianism]] — Bentham and Mill's proposal to judge actions by the happiness they produce.
- [[philosophy/existentialism|Existentialism]] — Kierkegaard, Sartre, and de Beauvoir on freedom, anxiety, and self-made meaning.
- [[philosophy/philosophy-of-mind|Philosophy of Mind]] — the mind-body problem, from Cartesian dualism to computational theories of thought.
- [[philosophy/ethics-of-technology|Ethics of Technology]] — how ancient ethical questions return in debates over [[computing/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].
