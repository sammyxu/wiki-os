# Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the effort to build machines that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, and using language. It is among the most ambitious threads in the [[computing/history-of-computing|history of computing]], repeatedly redefining what computers are for.

The field's intellectual roots trace to [[people/alan-turing|Alan Turing]], whose 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" proposed the imitation game — now called the Turing test — as a practical stand-in for the question of whether machines can think. The name "artificial intelligence" was coined by John McCarthy for the 1956 Dartmouth workshop, whose organizers included Marvin Minsky and [[people/claude-shannon|Claude Shannon]]. Whether symbol manipulation on a [[computing/turing-machine|Turing machine]] can amount to genuine understanding remains a central dispute in the [[philosophy/philosophy-of-mind|philosophy of mind]], sharpened by John Searle's 1980 Chinese Room argument.

## Booms, winters, and learning machines

Early symbolic AI produced expert systems but stalled twice in funding "winters" during the 1970s and late 1980s. Modern AI instead learns from data: the hardware gains described by [[computing/moores-law|Moore's Law]] made deep neural networks practical, yielding landmarks such as Deep Blue's 1997 chess victory over Garry Kasparov, AlexNet's 2012 image-recognition breakthrough, and AlphaGo's 2016 defeat of Lee Sedol.
