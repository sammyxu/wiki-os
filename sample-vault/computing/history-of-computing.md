# History of Computing

Computing grew from Victorian mechanical calculation into the networked, increasingly intelligent machines that now shape daily life. This hub traces that arc through three eras: theoretical foundations, the hardware revolution, and the rise of software and networks.

The story is not one of steady progress but of ideas waiting decades for technology to catch up — Charles Babbage sketched a programmable machine a century before electronics made one practical, and the dream of thinking machines predates the hardware that finally made it plausible.

## Foundations and Theory

- [[computing/analytical-engine|Analytical Engine]] — Babbage's 1837 design for a general-purpose mechanical computer, programmed with punched cards.
- [[computing/turing-machine|Turing Machine]] — Alan Turing's 1936 abstraction that defined what "computable" means.
- [[computing/enigma-codebreaking|Enigma Codebreaking]] — wartime cryptanalysis at Bletchley Park that pushed machines toward automation.
- [[computing/eniac|ENIAC]] — the 1945 electronic behemoth that proved digital computation at scale.
- [[computing/stored-program-concept|Stored-Program Concept]] — the von Neumann insight that programs are data, the blueprint for nearly every computer since.

## Hardware Revolutions

- [[computing/transistor|Transistor]] — Bell Labs' 1947 invention that replaced fragile vacuum tubes.
- [[computing/integrated-circuit|Integrated Circuit]] — many transistors on one chip, born in 1958–59.
- [[computing/moores-law|Moore's Law]] — the observation that transistor counts double roughly every two years.
- [[computing/personal-computer|Personal Computer]] — the 1970s shift that put computing on ordinary desks.

## Software and the Connected World

- [[computing/unix|Unix]] — the 1969 operating system whose ideas still underpin modern software.
- [[computing/arpanet|ARPANET]] — the 1969 packet-switched network that seeded the internet.
- [[computing/hypertext|Hypertext]] — linked documents, imagined long before the web existed.
- [[computing/world-wide-web|World Wide Web]] — Tim Berners-Lee's 1989 proposal that made the internet usable by everyone.
- [[computing/open-source-movement|Open Source Movement]] — collaborative development that now powers most infrastructure.
- [[computing/artificial-intelligence|Artificial Intelligence]] — from the 1956 Dartmouth workshop to modern machine learning.
