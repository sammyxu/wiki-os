# Hypertext

Hypertext is text that links directly to other text, letting readers move through ideas along paths of association rather than in a fixed linear order. The concept reshaped the [[computing/history-of-computing|history of computing]] and, eventually, how the world reads.

Vannevar Bush sketched the idea in his 1945 essay "As We May Think", imagining a desk-sized "memex" that stored documents connected by associative trails. Ted Nelson coined the word "hypertext" in 1965 for his sprawling Project Xanadu, and [[people/douglas-engelbart|Douglas Engelbart]] demonstrated working linked documents in his oN-Line System during the famous 1968 demo. Apple's HyperCard brought linking to personal computers in 1987, but it was [[people/tim-berners-lee|Tim Berners-Lee]] who gave hypertext planetary scale with the [[computing/world-wide-web|World Wide Web]].

## Linking as a way of thinking

The same associative principle drives modern note-taking. [[writing/digital-gardens|Digital gardens]] and the [[writing/zettelkasten|Zettelkasten]] method treat a personal knowledge base as a web of small linked notes, where connections between ideas matter as much as the ideas themselves.
