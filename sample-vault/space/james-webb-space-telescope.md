# James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is the largest and most powerful space observatory ever launched, designed to see the universe in infrared light. It lifted off on December 25, 2021, and began science operations in July 2022 from an orbit around the Sun–Earth L2 point, 1.5 million kilometers from Earth.

Built by NASA with ESA and the Canadian Space Agency, JWST carries a 6.5-meter segmented gold-coated mirror — nearly three times the diameter of the [[space/hubble-space-telescope|Hubble Space Telescope]]'s — shielded by a tennis-court-sized five-layer sunshield. Because the expansion of the universe stretches ancient light into the infrared, Webb can observe galaxies that formed only a few hundred million years after the [[science/big-bang-theory|Big Bang]], probing an era Hubble could barely glimpse. Its deep fields also exploit gravitational lensing, a consequence of [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]], to magnify the faintest early galaxies.

## Scientific goals

- Observe the first stars and galaxies in the early universe
- Study how galaxies assemble and evolve over cosmic time
- Watch stars and planetary systems form inside dusty clouds
- Analyze exoplanet atmospheres for water, carbon dioxide, and other molecules

Webb represents a generational leap in [[space/space-exploration|space exploration]], turning questions about cosmic origins into observational science.
