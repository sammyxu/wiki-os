# Mars Rovers

Mars rovers are robotic vehicles that drive across the Martian surface, acting as remote field geologists for scientists on Earth. Since the microwave-oven-sized Sojourner rolled off its lander in 1997, five NASA rovers have explored the planet, joined in 2021 by China's Zhurong.

The twin rovers Spirit and Opportunity landed in 2004 and found mineral evidence that liquid water once flowed on Mars; Opportunity survived until 2018, driving over 45 kilometers. Curiosity, a car-sized nuclear-powered laboratory, has climbed Mount Sharp in Gale Crater since 2012, while Perseverance has been caching rock samples in Jezero Crater since 2021 for a planned return to Earth. Because radio signals take up to 24 minutes each way, rovers rely on increasingly autonomous navigation software — a practical application of [[computing/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] in [[space/space-exploration|space exploration]].

## Notable milestones

- 1997: Sojourner, the first wheeled vehicle on another planet
- 2004: Spirit and Opportunity confirm a watery Martian past
- 2012: Curiosity's "seven minutes of terror" sky-crane landing
- 2021: Perseverance arrives with Ingenuity, the first Mars helicopter
