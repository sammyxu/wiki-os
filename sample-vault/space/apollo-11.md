# Apollo 11

Apollo 11 was the first crewed mission to land on the Moon, touching down in the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin spent about two and a half hours walking on the surface while Michael Collins orbited above in the command module Columbia.

The mission was the culmination of the [[space/apollo-program|Apollo Program]] and the decisive moment of the [[space/space-race|Space Race]], fulfilling President Kennedy's 1961 pledge to land a man on the Moon before the decade ended. Behind the three astronauts stood roughly 400,000 engineers, scientists, and technicians — among them [[people/margaret-hamilton]], whose onboard flight software famously recovered from computer overload alarms during the final descent, and [[people/katherine-johnson]], whose trajectory calculations helped make lunar rendezvous possible.

## The landing

During powered descent, the guidance computer threw 1202 and 1201 program alarms. Because Hamilton's team had designed the software to prioritize critical tasks, the landing continued safely. Armstrong took semi-manual control to avoid a boulder field, setting down with roughly 25 seconds of fuel margin remaining. The crew returned to Earth on July 24, splashing down in the Pacific.
