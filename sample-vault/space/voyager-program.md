# Voyager Program

The Voyager Program sent twin robotic probes, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, on a grand tour of the outer solar system beginning in 1977. Nearly five decades later, both spacecraft are still returning data from interstellar space, making them the most distant human-made objects ever built.

Launched to exploit a rare planetary alignment that occurs roughly every 176 years, the probes used gravity assists — a clever application of orbital mechanics related to the [[space/rocket-equation|rocket equation]] — to slingshot from Jupiter to Saturn, and in Voyager 2's case onward to Uranus and Neptune. Their discoveries included volcanoes on Io, the hazy atmosphere of Titan, and Neptune's Great Dark Spot. Each probe carries a Golden Record curated by a committee chaired by [[people/carl-sagan]], encoding sounds and images of Earth for any civilization that might one day find it.

## Legacy

Voyager 1 crossed into interstellar space in 2012, followed by Voyager 2 in 2018. Their faint signals, received through NASA's Deep Space Network, push the practical limits of [[systems/information-theory|information theory]]: data now trickles back at a few dozen bits per second across more than 20 light-hours.
