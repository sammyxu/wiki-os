# International Space Station

The International Space Station is a modular laboratory orbiting about 400 kilometers above Earth, continuously inhabited since November 2000. Built and operated by the United States, Russia, Europe, Japan, and Canada, it is the largest structure humans have ever assembled in space.

Construction began in 1998 with the Russian Zarya module and the American Unity node, joining rivals whose competition once defined the [[space/space-race|Space Race]] into a single cooperative project. Over more than 40 assembly flights, the station grew to the size of a football field, drawing on Russia's long experience with the Salyut and Mir stations — a lineage reaching back to [[space/sputnik|Sputnik]]. Crews of about seven conduct experiments in microgravity biology, materials science, and human physiology, knowledge essential for longer voyages of [[space/space-exploration|space exploration]].

## A platform for new spaceflight

Since 2012 the station has been resupplied by commercial vehicles, and since 2020 SpaceX's Crew Dragon has carried astronauts on [[space/reusable-rockets|reusable rockets]] — a shift from government-owned launchers to purchased services. NASA currently plans to operate the ISS through 2030 before transitioning to commercial stations.
