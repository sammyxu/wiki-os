# Space Exploration

Space exploration is humanity's effort to reach beyond Earth's atmosphere, from the first artificial satellite in 1957 to today's reusable boosters and deep-space observatories. This hub gathers the missions, machines, and ideas that made it possible.

## Foundations and the Race to Orbit

- [[space/rocket-equation|Rocket Equation]] — Tsiolkovsky's 1903 formula tying velocity to propellant mass; the math that governs every launch.
- [[space/sputnik|Sputnik]] — the polished Soviet sphere whose radio beeps opened the space age in October 1957.
- [[space/space-race|Space Race]] — the Cold War rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union that compressed decades of progress into a few years.
- [[space/apollo-program|Apollo Program]] — NASA's audacious effort to reach the Moon, guided by trajectory work from [[people/katherine-johnson|Katherine Johnson]].
- [[space/apollo-11|Apollo 11]] — the July 1969 mission that put Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the Sea of Tranquility.

## Robotic Explorers and Great Observatories

- [[space/voyager-program|Voyager Program]] — twin probes launched in 1977 that toured the outer planets and carried the Golden Record championed by [[people/carl-sagan|Carl Sagan]].
- [[space/mars-rovers|Mars Rovers]] — from Sojourner to Perseverance, wheeled geologists on the Red Planet.
- [[space/hubble-space-telescope|Hubble Space Telescope]] — the orbiting observatory that has sharpened our view of the cosmos since 1990.
- [[space/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] — Hubble's infrared successor, launched in 2021 to see the first galaxies.

## Living and Working in Space

- [[space/international-space-station|International Space Station]] — a continuously crewed orbital laboratory since November 2000.
- [[space/reusable-rockets|Reusable Rockets]] — boosters that land and fly again, collapsing the cost of reaching orbit in the 2010s.
