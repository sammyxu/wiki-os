# Apollo Program

The Apollo Program was NASA's human spaceflight effort of 1961 to 1972, which landed twelve astronauts on the Moon across six missions. It remains the only program ever to carry people beyond low Earth orbit.

Announced by President John F. Kennedy in May 1961 at the height of the [[space/space-race|Space Race]], Apollo chose the lunar-orbit rendezvous strategy and built the Saturn V, still the most powerful rocket ever flown successfully. After the tragic Apollo 1 fire in 1967, the program recovered to send Apollo 8 around the Moon in December 1968 and achieve the first landing with [[space/apollo-11|Apollo 11]] in July 1969.

## People and Machines

Roughly 400,000 people worked on Apollo. [[people/katherine-johnson|Katherine Johnson]] calculated and verified translunar trajectories at NASA, while [[people/margaret-hamilton|Margaret Hamilton]] led development of the Apollo Guidance Computer's onboard software, whose priority scheduling famously kept the Apollo 11 landing on track through computer alarms. The program's engineering legacy shaped decades of [[space/space-exploration|space exploration]].
