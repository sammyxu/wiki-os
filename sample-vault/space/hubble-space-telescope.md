# Hubble Space Telescope

The Hubble Space Telescope is a 2.4-meter orbital observatory launched aboard Space Shuttle Discovery in April 1990. Operating above the blur of Earth's atmosphere, it has produced some of the sharpest and most influential astronomical images ever taken.

Hubble's early years were nearly a disaster: a flaw in its primary mirror left images blurry until astronauts installed corrective optics during a daring 1993 servicing mission. Five shuttle visits in total kept the observatory at the frontier of science for decades. Its measurements of Cepheid variable stars pinned down the expansion rate of the universe, sharpening key evidence for the [[science/big-bang-theory|Big Bang theory]], while its deep-field exposures revealed thousands of galaxies in patches of seemingly empty sky. Hubble has also observed gravitational lensing, a striking confirmation of [[science/general-relativity|general relativity]], to map invisible dark matter.

## Scientific highlights

- The Hubble Deep Field and Ultra Deep Field, glimpses of the early universe
- Refining the Hubble constant, the expansion rate of the cosmos
- Evidence for supermassive black holes at the centers of galaxies
- Atmospheric studies of planets orbiting other stars
