# Rocket Equation

The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation, published by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky in 1903, relates the velocity change a rocket can achieve to its exhaust velocity and the ratio of its initial to final mass. It is the fundamental constraint on all spaceflight.

Because a rocket must carry its own propellant, the relation Δv = vₑ ln(m₀/m₁) is brutally exponential: modest gains in speed demand that enormous fractions of the vehicle be fuel. The result follows from conservation of momentum in [[science/newtonian-mechanics|Newtonian mechanics]], derived with the tools of [[science/calculus|calculus]] applied to a body of continuously changing mass. Engineers call its consequences the "tyranny of the rocket equation."

## Why It Matters

- It sets the payload fraction for every mission in [[space/space-exploration|space exploration]] — a typical orbital launcher is over 85 percent propellant.
- It explains staging: dropping empty tanks mid-flight resets the mass ratio in the rocket's favor.
- It motivates [[space/reusable-rockets|reusable rockets]] — since physics fixes the fuel bill, savings must come from flying the hardware again.
