# Sputnik

Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957, was the first artificial satellite to orbit Earth. Its steady radio beeps, audible to amateur operators worldwide, announced the opening of the space age.

The satellite was a polished aluminum sphere just 58 centimeters across and 83.6 kilograms in mass, lofted from the Baikonur Cosmodrome atop a modified R-7 intercontinental ballistic missile — a vehicle whose staged design reflected the logic of the [[space/rocket-equation|rocket equation]]. Circling the planet every 96 minutes, Sputnik transmitted for three weeks before its batteries died, and it burned up on reentry in January 1958.

## The Sputnik Crisis

The political shock in the United States was immediate and profound. The "Sputnik crisis" escalated the [[space/space-race|Space Race]], prompted the creation of NASA in 1958, and launched ARPA — the research agency whose networking projects later produced the [[computing/arpanet|ARPANET]]. Federal funding poured into science and engineering education, seeding a generation of researchers.
