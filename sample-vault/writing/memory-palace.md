# Memory Palace

The memory palace, or method of loci, is a mnemonic technique in which you place vivid images of the things you want to remember at locations along a familiar route, then retrieve them by mentally walking the route again.

Tradition credits the method to the Greek poet Simonides of Ceos in the fifth century BCE, and it was codified in Roman rhetoric manuals such as the anonymous *Rhetorica ad Herennium* and Cicero's *De Oratore*. Medieval scholars used palaces to hold sermons and scripture before paper was cheap; Joshua Foer's *Moonwalking with Einstein* (2011) documented its revival among modern memory athletes. In [[writing/knowledge-work]] the technique occupies the opposite pole from external systems like [[writing/commonplace-books]]: instead of writing ideas down, you architect a place in your head to keep them.

## Why it works

Spatial memory is unusually durable — we evolved to remember places and routes. The method converts abstract items into concrete, often absurd images, and the fixed sequence of locations preserves order, which lists of facts otherwise lack. Pairing palaces with [[writing/spaced-repetition]] keeps the routes from fading.
