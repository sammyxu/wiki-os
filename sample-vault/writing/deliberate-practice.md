# Deliberate Practice

Deliberate practice is structured, effortful training aimed at the edge of your current ability, with immediate feedback and repeated refinement. It is how experts get made, and it looks very different from casual repetition.

The concept comes from psychologist K. Anders Ericsson, whose 1993 study of violinists at the Berlin conservatory found that the best performers had accumulated thousands of hours of focused, coached practice — work later summarized in his book *Peak* (2016). Malcolm Gladwell's "10,000-hour rule" popularized (and oversimplified) the finding: Ericsson stressed that quality of practice matters more than raw hours. The tight loop of attempt, feedback, and correction is a classic example of [[systems/feedback-loops|feedback loops]] applied to human skill, and it applies as much to [[writing/knowledge-work]] as to music or chess.

## Applying it to thinking work

- Isolate one weak sub-skill (structuring an argument, summarizing a paper) rather than "practicing writing" in general.
- Seek fast, specific feedback from an editor, a reader, or your own future self via notes.
- Use [[writing/spaced-repetition]] to retain what practice teaches, and [[writing/writing-to-think|writing itself]] as the practice arena.
