# Evergreen Notes

Evergreen notes are written to be revised and accumulated across years, so that thinking compounds instead of starting over with every new project. The term was coined by researcher Andy Matuschak in his public working notes.

Matuschak, a former Apple engineer who later studied tools for thought with Michael Nielsen, contrasts evergreen notes with transient meeting notes and reading scraps: an evergreen note is concept-oriented, densely linked, and rewritten whenever your understanding improves. The practice inherits the one-idea rule of [[writing/atomic-notes]] and the linking discipline of the [[writing/zettelkasten]], but shifts the emphasis from capture to cultivation — the note is a draft of your best current thinking, not a record of someone else's.

## Cultivation over collection

Because evergreen notes are always presentable, many practitioners publish them directly as [[writing/digital-gardens]], letting readers watch ideas mature. Matuschak also links the practice to memory research, arguing that durable understanding needs both revision and review of the kind provided by [[writing/spaced-repetition]]. The test of an evergreen note is simple: a year later, does it still earn its place in your [[writing/knowledge-work]]?
