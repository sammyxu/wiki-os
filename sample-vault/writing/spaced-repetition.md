# Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition is a learning technique that schedules reviews of material at increasing intervals, timed to arrive just before you would otherwise forget. It turns memory from a matter of luck into a matter of scheduling.

The method rests on Hermann Ebbinghaus's 1885 discovery of the forgetting curve, which showed that retention decays predictably unless refreshed. Sebastian Leitner popularized a practical card-box system in the 1970s, and Piotr Woźniak's SuperMemo algorithm (1987) brought adaptive scheduling to software, later inspiring open-source tools like Anki (2006). For anyone doing serious [[writing/knowledge-work]], spaced repetition complements note-making systems such as the [[writing/zettelkasten]]: notes preserve ideas outside your head, while spaced review keeps the most important ones inside it.

## How the intervals work

- A correct recall pushes the next review further out (days, then weeks, then months).
- A failed recall resets the card to a short interval.
- Writing prompts as small, self-contained questions — much like [[writing/atomic-notes]] — makes them far easier to review.
